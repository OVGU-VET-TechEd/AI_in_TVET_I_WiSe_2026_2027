2.1 Adaptive Learning Systems 🧭

Learner model + domain model → next step.

🔧 In TVET: an online hydraulics module notices repeated errors with valve symbols and offers extra exercises before moving on.

🎯 Decides
next task, difficulty, pace
📊 Data
answers, errors, time on task
👩‍🏫 Teacher
sets goals, reads the flags, talks to the learner
Watch for (3 min)How does the video define adaptive learning? Which questions should you ask before choosing a platform?
An adaptive learning system has two models. The learner model estimates what the learner already knows. The domain model describes what there is to learn and in which order. Adaptation rules combine both and choose the next step. For the hydraulics example: the system decides about the next task, but the goals and the conversation with the learner stay with the teacher.

    Simplified, illustrative rules: 2 wrong in a row → hint and easier task; 3 wrong → worked example and a flag for the trainer. Real systems estimate mastery statistically from many answers – the logic "estimate → rule → next step" is the same.

    Answer a few tasks wrong in a row. With two wrong answers the system gives a hint and an easier task. With three, a good system stops and asks a human for help. Where would you set these thresholds for your apprentices?

    🎼 The teacher as conductor

    Good vocational teachers orchestrate – small groups, real work tasks, adaptive instructions, flexible arrangements (Zhao & Ko, 2024). Software must fit into this orchestration, not replace it.

    ⚠️ Risks

    biased learner modelscostsdata protection"learning in isolation"
    The evidence is promising but mostly from higher education, and many systems never leave the prototype stage. In vocational classrooms, the teacher orchestrates group work, real work tasks and instructions. Adaptive software is one instrument in this orchestra, not the conductor.

    Section 2.2 LO1

    2.2 Intelligent Tutoring Systems 🧑‍🏫

    An ITS guides the learner step by step through a task: it checks every step and gives hints – not only a score at the end.

    Outer loop
    which task next?    Task 1→Task 2→Task 3
    Inner loop
    within Task 2    Step 1→✔ feedback→Step 2→💡 hint→Step 2 again→✔ done

    Inner and outer loop (VanLehn, 2006)

    🧪 In the lab

    One-to-one tutoring was reported to be about 2 standard deviations better than classroom teaching – Bloom's "2 sigma problem" (Bloom, 1984). A review found step-based ITS almost as effective as human tutors (VanLehn, 2011):

    Human tutoring0.79
    Step-based ITS0.76
    d = 00.51.0

    🏫 In the field

    40 field studies show a complicated picture – few countries ("intelligent regional gap"), focus on test scores, some lack rigour (Wang et al., 2023).

    An open-source framework turns individual tutors into collaborative ones (Arnau-González et al., 2025).

    🏠 At home – watch forWhat does the AI tutor do differently from a search engine? Which of our three questions (decides · data · teacher) does the video answer – and which not?

    Critical report on the same tutor in schools: Meet Khanmigo – 60 Minutes (13 min) ↗

    An intelligent tutoring system works on two loops. The outer loop selects the next task, like an adaptive system. The inner loop is special: it follows every single step and gives a hint exactly where the learner is stuck. In the lab, step-based tutors come close to human tutors. In real schools the picture is more complicated. This gap between lab evidence and field evidence is something you will meet again and again.

    Chatbots are older than you might think. ELIZA, built by Joseph Weizenbaum in 1966, matched keywords with prepared answers. Today's chatbots are based on large language models and generate their answers freely – which makes them flexible, but also lets them invent facts. Jill Watson is an AI teaching assistant from Georgia Tech. While you watch, note which tasks it takes over and which remain with the human instructor.

    2.4 VR/AR and AI in Skill Training 🥽

    Conventional welding training is costly in material, time and infrastructure (Lavrentieva et al., 2020).

    🕶️ VR

    a fully virtual workshop · controller or torch replica

    👓 AR

    virtual workpiece, arc and seam in the real room · a real torch on a real workpiece holder

    🤖 Software / AI

    tracks torch angle, travel speed and distance, scores every seam in real time · trainers can replay each exercise

    🥽 VR/AR welding: safe, material-saving practice (Lavrentieva et al., 2020) · review of 42 studies: mostly entry-level, little evidence for distance learning (Chan et al., 2022)

    costscybersicknesstransfer to the real workshop

    Welding simulators are the best-known example of AI-supported skill training. Virtual reality puts you into a completely virtual workshop. Augmented reality shows virtual seams on top of the real environment, so you work with a real torch. The video is a vendor video, so watch it with a critical eye.     2.5 Predictive Analytics: How an Early-Warning System Works 🚦

    📁 Datagrades, attendance, background
    →
    🧠 Modellearns from past cohorts
    →
    📈 Risk score0.0 … 1.0 per learner
    →
    ⚖️ Thresholde.g. 0.5 – set by humans
    →
    🚩 Actionconversation, support offer

    Recall

    Of all learners who really drop out – how many does the system flag?

    Specificity

    Of all learners who stay – how many does it correctly leave alone?

    Precision

    Of all flagged learners – how many are really at risk?

    Accuracy

    Of all decisions – how many are correct?

    ⚖️ The threshold is a human decision: lower → more dropouts caught, but more false alarms.
    An early-warning system turns data into a risk score. Humans decide on a threshold above which a learner is flagged. And then humans act: a conversation, a support offer. Four terms help you to read the numbers. Recall asks how many real dropouts are found. Precision asks how many flagged learners are really at risk. Accuracy mixes everything – and that is the trap on the next slide.

    2.5 Predictive Analytics: The Accuracy Trap 🪤

    14,495 students, dropout rate 8.5 %: a standard classifier recognised only 13 % of dropouts – while being 99 % right on those who stayed (Gonzalez-Nucamendi et al., 2023).

    Presets:

    Calculator for illustration, using the study's sample size and dropout rate. In the study, adjusting the decision threshold raised dropout precision above 50 %. "Lower threshold" uses illustrative values.

    With the original values, accuracy looks great at over ninety percent. But a system that flags nobody at all is almost as accurate. Move the recall slider up and watch what happens to false alarms – and try the presets "flag nobody" and "flag everybody". This is why accuracy alone tells you very little about an early-warning system.

    Section 2.7 LO2

    2.7 Current Development: AI Agents 🦾

    💬 Chatbot

    one question → one answer

    🦾 Agent

    one goal → plans steps, uses tools, checks results, tries again (remember P03)

    ✋ Humans set the goal and approve the result.

    🔧 In TVET (illustrative)

    • Tutor agent – plans an apprentice's revision week from the errors in the tutoring log and schedules matching tasks
    • Course-building agent – drafts learning nuggets step by step, e.g. the open-source LiaScript teaching-agent ↗: outline → didactics → agenda → sessions → materials, with approval at every step
    ⚠️ The more autonomy, the more human oversightAgents act – a wrong step is repeated quickly and at scale. High-risk AI systems must allow effective human oversight (EU AI Act, Art. 14; European Union, 2024).
    🏠 At home – watch forHow does the video describe the step from a single model to a "compound AI system" – and what does the agent add?
    The newest development is AI agents. A chatbot answers one question. An agent receives a goal, plans several steps, uses tools such as web search or files, observes the result and adjusts its plan. In vocational education, agents could plan revision for apprentices or build learning material. The LiaScript teaching-agent, for example, builds a course in phases and asks the author for approval at each phase. Because agents act on their own, a mistake is not one wrong answer – it can be a chain of wrong actions. That is why human oversight matters even more.
